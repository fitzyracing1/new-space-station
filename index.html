Ping Lite · Open concept · v0.2 · Oct 2026

A new commercial crewed space station for life after the ISS

Ping Lite is a six-person, permanently crewed laboratory, factory and home in low Earth orbit (4 government + 2 commercial berths). It is built and tested on the ground as one integrated station, launched on a single Starship, and designed in the open. Read the design, then tell us what you'd change.

Inside the station

One 7.2 m aluminium pressure vessel with five 2.2 m decks, a forward docking node and an aft service section. It is fully outfitted on the ground and sized to Starship's 8 m payload envelope.

NODE

Forward node

3 IDSS docking ports for crew vehicles (so 3 can be docked during a crew handover) and 2 berthing ports for cargo vehicles and growth. It can be sealed off from the rest of the station.

~2.6 m × 2.5 m · plus aft IDSS for cargo and reboost

Systems & service section

Launch & assembly plan

One launch replaces v0.1's five, so there is no assembly campaign. The trade-off is that the whole program depends on Starship (its payload capability and payload-bay door are open risks). A 5-Falcon modular design is kept as the fallback.

Design highlights

  • Regenerative ECLSS sized for 6 crew (10 peak): ≥95% water recovery rising to ≥98%, and ~50% O₂ recovery. This saves ~10 t/yr of upmass.
  • Two-fault tolerance against catastrophic hazards where practical, in line with NASA human-rating practice.
  • Lifeboat rule: docked return capacity is always at least the number of crew on board.
  • Hall-effect electric propulsion for routine drag makeup, with chemical reserve for debris avoidance.
  • Pressure-tight isolation zones inside the single hull, so a leak doesn't force evacuation of the whole station.
  • ≥1 Gbps payload downlink via optical relay links.
  • 15-year structural design life with replaceable arrays, batteries and ECLSS units.

The NASA proposal angle

NASA plans to buy services from commercially owned stations to keep U.S. astronauts continuously in low Earth orbit after the ISS. Its Commercial LEO Destination Contract (CLDC) Final RFP (solicitation 80JSC027R0004) was released on 9 Oct 2026, and proposals are due 8 Dec 2026.

Where this concept fits

  • NASA asks for a station that supports at least 4 crew, including 2 government astronauts, with continuous presence (no more than a 14-day gap between crews). Ping Lite has 6 permanent berths (4 government + 2 commercial) and docking room for 3 crew vehicles during handovers.
  • NASA allows a phased path from crew-tended (Mission A) to continuous crew (Mission B) to Standard Service. Ping Lite launches with Category 3 science hardware already installed, so moving from Mission A to Standard Service is a matter of certification, not more launches.
  • The first 4 missions must use already-certified crew and cargo vehicles. The baseline is Crew Dragon, Cargo Dragon and Cygnus, on 4 IDSS docking ports and 2 berthing ports.
  • U.S. launch vehicles: one Starship for the station. NASA's launch-vehicle category rules for Starship are still an open item.

Open challenges, where we want your input

  • Schedule: a single launch helps, but the realistic launch date is about Q4 2032. Even the compressed option (launch Q4 2030, first crew in early 2031) arrives just after the ISS's planned end-2030 retirement, and we rate it unlikely. Should we partner?
  • Starship risk: the station is ~93 t, while recent test flights carried about 35–40 t. The whole station rides on one launch, and how it gets out of the payload bay is still open.
  • Team and past performance: as a new entrant we need experienced partners. We have no agreements yet.
  • Certification basis: building a compliance matrix against NASA's commercial LEO requirements and standards.
